## Language

- **Primary Language:** English (US-centric). The interface, dominant culture, and user base are English-speaking.
- **Multilingual Strategy:** Content in other languages (e.g., Japanese, French) is accepted but rare. It may limit reach as the platform lacks translation features. To maximize visibility, write titles and descriptions in English, even if linked content is in another language. Do not use language tags or sub-community notes; the platform has no such features.
- **Style:** Poetic, concise, and descriptive. Use evocative, sensory language (e.g., "Soft light, raw texture, quiet mornings") rather than instructional or conversational phrasing. Avoid internet slang, memes, or overly casual tones. The tone should match a high-end design publication—informative but tasteful.

## Good Fit

- Visual tools or libraries: Projects with strong visual output (e.g., UI component libraries, image processing tools, generative art frameworks, data visualization libraries) that can be showcased through high-quality screenshots or GIFs.
- Design systems and themes: CSS frameworks, design tokens, icon sets, or color palette generators that lend themselves to curated, aesthetic presentations.
- Minimalist or niche applications: Apps or tools that embody a "quiet luxury" aesthetic—clean, intentional, and focused (e.g., a note-taking app, a meditation timer, a photo editor).
- Curated content projects: Collections of links, resources, or inspirations (e.g., a list of free design assets, a bookmarks manager, a digital garden) that can be organized into thematic collections.
- Portfolio or showcase projects: Personal websites, portfolio templates, or demo reels that are visually stunning and can be presented as a single, high-quality image with a short caption.

## Poor Fit

- Command-line tools or APIs: Projects that are purely backend, text-based, or have no visual component (e.g., a CLI tool, a database library, a web scraper). They cannot be represented effectively on a visual-first platform.
- Documentation-heavy projects: Projects that require long, instructional text or multiple paragraphs to explain (e.g., a complex framework, a scientific paper, a detailed tutorial). Cosmos does not support long-form content.
- Coding bootcamps or courses: Educational content that relies on step-by-step instructions, code blocks, or interactive elements. The platform is not designed for learning.
- Humor or meme-based projects: Projects that rely on internet culture, jokes, or viral trends. The platform's tone is serious and tasteful.
- Projects with low-quality or no visuals: Any project that cannot produce a single high-resolution, aesthetically pleasing image or GIF. Screenshots of code editors or terminal outputs are not appropriate.

## Format And Style Notes

- Use high-resolution, single-focus images. Avoid cluttered or text-heavy images. The image should be the primary content, with text as a secondary caption.
- Write short, evocative titles (2–6 words). Titles should be poetic and descriptive, not functional or SEO-driven. Avoid hashtags and full sentences.
- Keep descriptions to 1–3 sentences. Use evocative, sensory language. Avoid bullet points, lists, or instructional text. Focus on the feeling or aesthetic of the linked content.
- Always embed the source URL for images. Attribution is core to the platform's culture. If you post an image, link to the original source or your own portfolio. Never post unattributed content.
- Group related items into "Collections." Each collection should have a thematic title and a 1-sentence description. Collections are the primary way to organize multiple pieces of content.
- Avoid Markdown syntax. Cosmos uses a rich-text editor that does not render Markdown. Do not use `#`, `*`, ``, or other Markdown characters. Use the editor's built-in formatting (bold, italic, links) instead.
- Use a neutral to warm tone. Avoid internet slang, memes, sarcasm, or overly casual phrasing. The tone should be respectful, tasteful, and aspirational.
- Prioritize aesthetic over utility. The platform is for showcasing beauty and taste, not for solving problems or explaining functionality. Choose images and links that are visually pleasing, even if they don't fully represent the project's features.
