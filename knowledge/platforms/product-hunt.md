## Language

The primary language is **English (American English)**. Titles, taglines, descriptions, and comments should use US English spellings and vocabulary. While the platform has a global audience, English is the lingua franca for reaching the widest possible user base. Non-English posts are rare and will significantly limit reach and engagement. If your project is non-English, consider making a strong English-language case for it, or target a sub-community if one exists. Use clear, benefit-driven language. Avoid marketing fluff and technical jargon that isn't commonly understood by early adopters.

## Good Fit

- A polished, consumer-facing SaaS product with a clear, single problem-solution fit. Example: A new time-tracking tool with a unique, elegant UI.
- A developer tool or API that solves a painful, common engineering problem. Example: A new GraphQL client, a simpler CI/CD pipeline tool, or a library for handling state.
- A mobile app with a novel interaction pattern or a specific, underserved use case. Example: A minimalist habit tracker with a unique gamification system.
- A no-code or low-code platform that empowers non-technical users. Example: A tool to build internal dashboards without SQL.
- An AI-powered tool that offers a clear, demonstrable improvement over existing workflows. Example: An AI that summarizes meeting notes and extracts action items.

## Poor Fit

- A simple landing page or a "coming soon" page with no working product. Product Hunt is for launched products, not ideas.
- An enterprise-focused, highly complex platform that requires a demo and a sales team to understand. Example: A new ERP system or a niche supply chain management tool.
- A content or media product (e.g., a blog, a podcast, a newsletter). These belong on platforms like Substack or Medium.
- A project that is a blatant copy of an existing, well-known product with no clear differentiator. Example: "Uber for dog walking" with no unique angle.
- An open-source library with no user-facing interface and no clear "product" story. While some libraries launch, they need a strong narrative and a demo.

## Format And Style Notes

- Tagline must be a complete, benefit-driven sentence (≤60 chars).
- Description expands on the tagline with a problem-solution-differentiator structure.
- Use the "First Comment" for your long-form pitch.
- Thumbnail/Logo should be a high-resolution square, text-free if possible.
- Select 3-5 highly relevant topics/tags.
- Engage directly and quickly in the comments, especially in the first hour.
- Avoid over-hyping and marketing fluff.
- Show, don't just tell. Use a demo GIF or short video in your first comment.

## Tone

A clean, high-energy launch pad for polished, utility-first products; be direct, be visual, and be ready to defend your decisions.
