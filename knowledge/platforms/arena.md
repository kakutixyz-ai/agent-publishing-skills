## Good Fit

- Your project has a strong visual component—screenshots, diagrams, UI mockups, architecture sketches, concept art, or data visualizations.
- Your project explores abstract, conceptual, or research-driven themes—speculative design, experimental interfaces, critical theory, or artistic technology.
- Your project's value is communicated through mood, atmosphere, or aesthetic rather than feature lists or performance benchmarks.
- Your project is open-ended or unfinished—Arena thrives on process, sketches, and works-in-progress, not polished final products.
- Your project has source material (books, films, other projects) that inspired it—you can create a channel that traces these influences visually.

## Poor Fit

- Your project is a utility or tool with no visual identity (e.g., a command-line library, a backend framework, a configuration file generator).
- Your project requires detailed technical documentation to be understood—Arena cannot display code blocks, tables, or structured markdown.
- Your project is commercial or sales-driven—Arena's culture actively rejects marketing, metrics, and calls-to-action.
- Your project is a step-by-step tutorial—Arena is not a how-to platform; it is a space for inspiration and reference.
- Your project relies on text-heavy explanations—if a single image or a 2-line caption cannot convey the core idea, Arena is the wrong venue.

## Format And Style Notes

- Channel titles are short, evocative, and title-cased or lowercase. Avoid ALL CAPS and complete sentences.
- Block descriptions are one line or less. Use this space for citations, keywords, or a single poetic observation—never a paragraph.
- Thumbnail images are high-impact and high-resolution. The first block in your channel acts as its cover. Choose one image that encapsulates the entire channel's thesis.
- Mix block types to create rhythm. Alternate between full-bleed images, text screenshots, linked articles, and embedded PDFs. Do not post 50 consecutive images of the same subject.
- Text is uploaded as images when typography matters. If you want to share a paragraph from a book, a poem, or a code comment, screenshot it as a PNG/JPG to preserve its visual character.
- Channel descriptions are 1–2 sentences maximum. Use this space for a manifesto, a provocation, or a source note—not a summary.
- Embrace ambiguity and open-endedness. Do not explain everything. Leave room for viewers to make their own connections.
- Attribute sources in the block description, not in a separate credit block. Keep attribution brief and consistent.
