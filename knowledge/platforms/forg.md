## Trigger Context

Triggered by mentions of 'Forg', 'forg.to', or 'build in public platform'

## Language

The primary language is **English** (American English with technical jargon). The platform's core audience is the global "Build in Public" community, centered around English-speaking technical founders, indie hackers, and engineers. Non-English posts are rare and typically receive less engagement. If posting in another language, include an English summary and use language tags where supported. The tone is casual, direct, and technical — use first-person ("I shipped this"), active voice, and avoid marketing speak. Emojis are acceptable but used sparingly (e.g., ✅ for done, 🔥 for hot fix). Domain-specific acronyms (MVP, DAU, LTV, PR, CI/CD) are expected without explanation.

## Good Fit

- Early-stage software projects (pre-v1.0) where the creator is actively iterating based on user feedback
- Open-source libraries or tools with a clear changelog and visible development activity
- SaaS products or web apps with measurable metrics (users, revenue, retention) to share publicly
- Technical tutorials or walkthroughs that document the build process step-by-step
- Failed experiments or pivots — the community values learning from mistakes as much as successes

## Poor Fit

- Polished, finished products with no ongoing development or updates to share
- Non-technical projects (physical products, services, creative works without a code component)
- Marketing-heavy content focused on driving sales rather than sharing the build process
- Projects with no public-facing metrics or measurable progress (e.g., "I'm thinking about building something")
- Content that requires significant explanation of basic concepts (the audience is technical and expects depth)

## Format And Style Notes

- Lead with a date or version number. Every post should have a clear timestamp or iteration marker.
- Keep paragraphs short (1-3 lines max). Walls of text are skipped.
- Use bullet lists for tasks and metrics. Makes the post scannable.
- Embed screenshots or GIFs directly. Visuals of the actual UI/UX are expected.
- Use inline code for technical terms. Commands, variable names, and code snippets belong in backticks.
- End with a direct question or call to action. Drive engagement.
- Avoid introductions and inspirational quotes. Get straight to the update.
- Share failures and metrics honestly. The community respects transparency over polish.

## Tone

Write like a technical founder sharing their unfiltered build log to a room of peer engineers — direct, data-driven, and humble about the struggle.
