## Trigger Context

Triggered by mentions of 'Behance', 'portfolio platform', 'design community', 'creative showcase', or 'Adobe portfolio'.

## Language

**Primary Language:** English (American English preferred for project titles, descriptions, and tags). The platform's default UI and the vast majority of highly-viewed projects are in English.

## Good Fit

- Polished visual design projects — Branding systems, UI/UX mockups, logo explorations, packaging design, illustration series, photography portfolios.
- Process-driven creative work — Projects that include mood boards, sketches, iterations, or behind-the-scenes documentation alongside final deliverables.
- Portfolio-ready professional work — Client work, competition entries, or speculative projects presented with a high level of finish and narrative.
- Motion and interactive design — Animated prototypes, video demos, or interactive UI flows (supports native video uploads and YouTube/Vimeo embeds).
- Tool and technique showcases — Projects demonstrating mastery of specific tools (e.g., Figma, Blender, After Effects, Procreate) or techniques (e.g., 3D modeling, typography, data visualization).

## Poor Fit

- Raw code repositories or open-source libraries — Behance is not a place to host GitHub-style READMEs or code documentation; it is a visual-first portfolio.
- Unfinished or low-effort work — WIP sketches, rough drafts, or projects with poor resolution images are poorly received.
- Non-visual projects — Pure text essays, audio-only content, or data-heavy reports without strong visual presentation.
- Promotional or sales-heavy content — Direct advertisements, affiliate links, or spammy "hire me" posts without substantial visual work.
- Generic stock content — Projects that appear to be simple compilations of stock assets without original creative input.

## Format And Style Notes

- Lead with your strongest visual. The cover image is the thumbnail that determines whether users click through. It must be high-resolution (1920px wide minimum), well-composed, and representative of the entire project.
- Tell a story with images, not text. Use the "Image Grid" module to sequence your work in a logical flow (problem → process → solution). Text should be short captions under images, not long paragraphs.
- Include a process section. Behance users appreciate seeing how you arrived at the final result. Add sketches, mood boards, iterations, or user flows later in the project.
- Use consistent image sizing and quality. Upload images at 1400px to 2000px wide (72 DPI). Avoid very tall vertical images that require excessive scrolling. Ensure all images are sharp and properly exposed.
- Write a concise project overview. Begin with 2–3 sentences stating the client, objective, and your role. This sets context for the viewer.
- Credit collaborators and tools. End with a "Credits" or "Tools Used" section listing team members, software (e.g., Figma, Adobe Illustrator, Blender), and fonts used. This is standard practice and builds credibility.
- Tag strategically. Use 5–10 relevant tags that describe the project's discipline, style, and tools. Tags drive discoverability in Behance's search and recommendation engine.
- Keep the overall project length digestible. Aim for 8–15 images total. Too few images (1–3) feel incomplete; too many (30+) overwhelm the viewer.

## Tone

Professional, quiet, and visually competitive — present your work as a curated gallery experience where every image earns its place.
