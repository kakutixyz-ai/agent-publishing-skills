## Trigger Context

Triggered by mentions of 'Medium', 'publishing on Medium', 'Medium article', or 'tech blog platform'.

## Language

The primary language for Medium's tech and industry insights audience is **English (US)**. Content is expected to be in professional but conversational English. Non-English posts are possible but will have significantly reduced reach unless they target a specific, established Medium publication in that language. Code snippets, technical terms, and industry jargon are acceptable and expected. Avoid slang and overly casual language. If English is not your first language, ensure grammar and flow are polished, as readers are discerning.

## Good Fit

- Data-driven technical deep-dives: Blog posts explaining a performance optimization, architecture decision, or algorithm choice with concrete metrics (e.g., "How we cut API latency by 60%").
- Lessons learned from building a product: Honest post-mortems, scaling war stories, or retrospectives with actionable advice.
- Tool or library introductions with a narrative: Announcing an open-source tool, but framed as "Why we built X" rather than a dry changelog.
- Framework or methodology comparisons: Objective, data-backed comparisons between different technical approaches (e.g., "React vs. Vue in 2024: A real-world performance test").
- Career and industry insights for technical practitioners: Posts on engineering culture, team dynamics, technical leadership, or career growth from a practitioner's perspective.

## Poor Fit

- Pure product announcements or release notes: "Version 2.0 is out!" with no story or insight behind it.
- Clickbait or listicles: "10 Ways to Code Faster" without substantive content or data.
- Overly academic or research papers: Dense, citation-heavy text without practical takeaways.
- Simple tutorials without context: "How to install X" without explaining *why* the reader should care or the problem it solves.
- Aggressive sales or promotional content: Direct calls to "Buy now" or "Sign up for my course" without providing value first.

## Format And Style Notes

- Lead with a specific problem or question.
- Use short, scannable paragraphs (2-4 sentences max).
- Use data and metrics to back up every claim.
- Include a "Key Takeaways" or "TL;DR" section at the top or bottom.
- Use images that add information, not decoration.
- Write in first-person ("we" or "I") to build credibility and narrative.
- End with a reflective question or invitation for discussion, not a sales pitch.
- Use blockquotes sparingly, only for direct quotes or to highlight a single, powerful insight.

## Tone

Write like a thoughtful, experienced engineer sharing a hard-won lesson over coffee—data-driven, humble, and focused on the reader's takeaway.
