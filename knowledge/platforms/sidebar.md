## Language

- **Primary language:** English (US/International technical English). All submissions, blurbs, and the site itself are in English.
- **Multilingual strategy:** Only English-language content is accepted. Non-English resources, even with English translations, are unlikely to be selected. The audience is global but expects fluent, native-level technical English.
- **Tone:** Neutral, factual, precise. Avoid marketing fluff, exclamation points, or casual slang.

## Good Fit

- In-depth technical tutorials that solve a specific, non-trivial frontend problem (e.g., "How to implement virtual scrolling in React")
- Well-researched comparisons of frontend tools or techniques (e.g., "CSS Container Queries vs. Media Queries")
- Case studies of real-world frontend architecture decisions (e.g., "How we reduced bundle size by 40%")
- Original research or data-driven articles about frontend performance, accessibility, or UX
- Thoughtful essays on frontend development practices, patterns, or philosophy

## Poor Fit

- Beginner-level tutorials (e.g., "What is JavaScript?")
- Opinion pieces without technical substance or data
- Product launches, announcements, or release notes for libraries/tools
- Content focused on backend, DevOps, or non-frontend topics
- Clickbait titles, listicles, or "10 tips" style articles

## Format And Style Notes

- Lead with the problem, not the solution. The blurb should first state what challenge the content addresses, then briefly mention the approach.
- Be specific and technical. Use precise terminology and avoid vague praise.
- State the target audience explicitly. Help the curator and readers understand who benefits most.
- Keep blurbs to 1–3 sentences. Every word must earn its place. No filler.
- Avoid marketing language and hype words. No "amazing," "incredible," "game-changing," "must-read."
- Focus on the "why" and "so what." Explain why the content matters beyond the obvious.
- Use neutral, factual language. Let the content speak for itself. Avoid editorializing.
- Match the title to the actual article title. Do not rewrite it for clicks.
