## Good Fit

- Is a deployable software tool, library, or framework with measurable impact (e.g., stars, downloads, performance gains).
- Has a clear "Work" section narrative: what problem it solves, how it was built, and what results it achieved.
- Includes visual proof: screenshots, architecture diagrams, or demo GIFs that fit Peerlist's card-based layout.
- Targets a professional developer audience (e.g., backend, DevOps, frontend, data engineering).
- Has open-source components or public repositories that can be linked for verification.

## Poor Fit

- Is a purely artistic or design portfolio (use Dribbble or Behance instead).
- Is a non-technical business or service (e.g., consulting, coaching, or e-commerce store).
- Lacks any public metrics, code, or deployable artifact (e.g., only conceptual or theoretical).
- Requires long-form narrative or storytelling (use Medium or a personal blog).
- Is a finished product with no ongoing development or maintenance (Peerlist rewards active builders).

## Format And Style Notes

- Lead with a metric. Every project description should start with a number or percentage.
- Use strong action verbs. Start each bullet with "Built," "Led," "Optimized," "Architected," "Migrated."
- Keep descriptions under 3 sentences. Peerlist profiles are scanned, not read.
- Avoid emojis in "Work" sections. Emojis are acceptable in the Bio line only.
- Link every claim. Every metric or achievement should have a supporting URL.
- Use consistent tense. Past tense for completed projects, present tense for ongoing ones.
- Lead with the problem, not the solution. Show you understand the *why* before the *what*.
- Use white space liberally. Separate sections with blank lines; avoid dense paragraphs.

## Tone

"Show your impact with hard numbers and ship stories; Peerlist rewards builders who prove their worth through concrete, verifiable outcomes."
